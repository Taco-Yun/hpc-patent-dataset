High-performance computing patent dataset with full text, structured summaries and semantic embeddings

HPC-PatentTrace covers 6,535 United States granted patents, filed from 2015 to 2025, that mention high-performance computing. This page shows how the corpus was built and what the release contains.

Every number below is read from release/ in the output folder of the step 6 pipeline notebook (colab/step6_patentsum_pipeline.ipynb) by scripts/build_results_page.py of this repository.

Corpus construction

The query returned 21,947 publication numbers. The homonym screening removes 5,513 records in which HPC stands for another term, for example hydroxypropyl cellulose or a high-pressure compressor. The completeness filter keeps the 7,158 candidates whose title, abstract, background, detailed description and claims were all extracted. The inclusion rule then keeps the 6,535 records whose text contains the expanded phrase high performance comput*.

21,947 is the number of records in the crawler output (step 5), one per publication number.

Where the rules remove records

Both rules keep most records of sections G (physics, including computing) and H (electricity) and remove most records of the other sections. The completeness filter removes 40.1 percent of the G candidates and 42.3 percent of H, against 90.1 percent of A and 94.6 percent of C. The inclusion rule then removes 5.1 percent of G and 4.5 percent of H, against 57.2 percent of A and 84.4 percent of C, the sections where the other meanings of HPC belong.

A candidate is removed by the completeness filter mostly because its detailed description (48.0 percent of all candidates) or its background (39.5 percent) was not extracted from the patent page.

Summary layers

Each record has three facets (background, invention, technical detail), written by Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and three summaries, written by Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct with the same final call. The synthesized layer is written from the title, the abstract and the facets after a reasoning step. direct-brief reads short excerpts of the sections in one prompt; direct-full reads the same section budgets as the facets in one prompt.

Topics

SPECTER2 encodes the title and the synthesized summary of each record. UMAP reduces the vectors to three dimensions, HDBSCAN finds 51 clusters, and the clusters are merged into 25 topics that cover 5,033 records (77.0 percent); 1,502 records (23.0 percent) belong to no cluster. Each point of the map is one record. Choose a topic to highlight it, or colour the records by class section; drag to rotate.

The topics agree with the main four-character class of the records clearly above chance and far from completely: normalised mutual information 0.456, adjusted Rand index 0.146, homogeneity 0.477 and completeness 0.436. The topics are a semantic index next to the patent classification, not a replacement for it.

Ownership and litigation

For 763 records (11.7 percent) the current assignee on the patent page differs from the original assignee. The latest assignment record transfers ownership (assignment, change of name or merger) in 417 of them (54.7 percent). Of the others, 180 have no assignment record, and the latest record of 139 is a security interest or its release, of 21 a correction and of 6 another type. ownership_events.csv gives these fields for every record. Its column effective_assignee keeps the original assignee when no transfer supports the change; with it, 404 records (6.2 percent) change owner.

Technical validation

Pairing check. Each summary is scored against the abstract of its own record and against the abstract shifted by one position. ROUGE-1 F1 is 0.383 to 0.392 against the own abstract and 0.188 to 0.198 against the shifted one. Over the three layers, 143 summaries (72 of them synthesized) score no higher against their own abstract than against the shifted one, or below 0.10; they are listed in pairing_integrity_flags.csv.

CPC classification. A logistic regression on each unit-length embedding predicts the four-character CPC codes of a record (63 codes with at least 20 records, multi-label), with a split that keeps patent families together (1,325 test records). All five representations reach a micro-F1 between 0.605 and 0.619; the synthesized layer is 0.014 above the abstract (paired bootstrap with 1,000 draws, 95 percent interval 0.004 to 0.023).

Original assignee. The same classifier predicts the original assignee among the 36 assignees with at least 20 records. Accuracy stays between 0.529 and 0.568, against 21.3 percent for always choosing the largest assignee. This is a usage example, not evidence for one representation.
